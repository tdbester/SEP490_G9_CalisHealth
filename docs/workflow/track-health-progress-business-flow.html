How to read it

Three lanes again: what the Member does, what CalisHealth does with it, and which tables the numbers actually come from. The whole flow is a loop — every workout writes fresh rows, and those same rows feed every card on the tracking screens.

Part 1 — A workout becomes data

It starts like any other workout: the member picks something to train and the camera on their phone counts the reps while they do it. What gets sent to the server is deliberately plain — the raw counts per set, nothing more. The server always recomputes the clean percentage itself, so a buggy or tampered client can never inflate a score.

The session is stored, then every exercise, then every set. From there the progression rules are checked: did enough sets reach the required clean reps (or clean seconds, for holds), and was the overall clean rate high enough? When a rule passes, the next exercise unlocks and the member's personal bests are updated. That single moment is what the whole tracking area is later built on.

Part 2 — The health overview

One screen shows the health score as a dial, the current body metrics with the change since the start of the month, and six months of BMI in one chart. The score itself is a weighted mix of consistency (30%), form accuracy (30%), training streak (20%) and BMI (20%). A part with no data is left out and the remaining weights are shared out, so the score never quietly invents a number it doesn't have.

If the member never logged a measurement, the system quietly borrows theirs from the onboarding survey rather than showing an empty screen. And if that survey is still unfinished, a friendly banner on the same page invites them to finish it — because their answers are exactly what feeds this screen.

Part 3 — History, analytics and streak

The archive lists every completed session and can be narrowed to a day or a workout type. Opening one gives the full log: every exercise, its sets, its reps or hold seconds and its accuracy — and the server checks the session really belongs to that member before showing a single detail.

Performance analytics goes further: the form errors AI coaching flagged most often, the clean rep ratio, the clean hold ratio, the volume progression, and how the time splits between active work and rest. The buckets follow the range — weeks for a month, months for a quarter, quarters for a year — so the chart always matches the summary cards above it. The closing insight is written from the member's own numbers, never from a borrowed benchmark.

And the day streak ties it together: a calendar of training days, the current run, the personal best, and how far along the weekly goal they are. Tapping any day opens that day's report.
